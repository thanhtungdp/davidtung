The hypothetical of SpaceX buying Cursor for $60B sounds absurd if you only see a "coding tool". Seen through the lens of the **valuation story**, it makes a lot of sense.

## Buying AI to use it, or to tell a story?

A space-infrastructure company is valued on launch cadence and contracts. A company with core AI capability is valued on the **learning speed** of the whole organization. Those two multiples are far apart.

- Buying capability: shorten software cycles for thousands of engineers.
- Buying a story: the market re-rates the whole company on an AI multiple.
- Buying data: understand how engineers work to build internal agents.

## Three questions every CEO should ask

| Question | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| Which unit economics does AI change? | Cost per transaction, time per order |
| Who owns the data after integration? | Long-term advantage |
| Can the new story be proven with numbers? | Market trust |

## Lessons for small and mid-sized companies

You don't need to buy an AI company. You need **one core workflow running on AI** that customers can see. That's the most credible story there is.
